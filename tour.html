Follow one student through it

Maya is a sample profile, not a real person. Everything below is the actual output LJSportMind produces for her profile today. You can see the same example on the home page.

1Maya builds her Sports Passport

She enters what she has actually done: she's a college student who wants to break into sports media, with writing and playing experience listed as skills and journalism and content creation as interests. Her achievements:

Wrote articles for the campus newspaper sports section
Ran the club soccer team's Instagram account
Played four years of club soccer

That's her Passport: one profile and evidence record, saved on her device. She never has to explain her background again.

2She sees her career matches, with reasons

Matches compare her Passport with the typical skills of 8 curated sports career paths. They're guidance, not a validated assessment or a hiring prediction.

3She turns gaps into a development plan

She taps Make this my target role on Sports Journalist / Broadcaster. Her plan is saved with her Passport:

0 of 5 steps done

  • Build Broadcasting / On-Camera: get one project or experience that shows it, then add it to your achievements.
  • Start a match-report or postgame recap series in your own voice.
  • Add an achievement that proves one of your matching skills.
  • Compare one real job posting for this role.
  • Apply to one Sports Journalist / Broadcaster opening using the job links.

When she later adds on-camera experience to her Passport, the first step ticks itself and shows "now in your Passport". She ticks the others herself. Next time she opens LJSportMind, she lands on "Welcome back, Maya. Your plan: 1 of 5 steps done."

Next to the plan, Skills that open the most doors shows which missing skills appear across her top roles, so she can see which one helps most.

4She checks a real job posting

She pastes a posting she found: "Digital Content Coordinator. Write match previews and articles, manage Instagram and TikTok, edit short videos in Premiere, report on engagement data in Excel."

She matches 2 of 4 skills this posting mentions. Closest of her career paths: Sports Content Creator.

One tap on Add missing skills to my plan adds Video Editing and Data & Analytics as plan steps. It's based on keywords in the posting, so treat it as a guide.

5She asks her AI Coach about her plan

The AI Coach works in context. It can "Explain this match" for a specific role, help with a specific plan step, or say how to show evidence of a missing skill. It only uses facts from her Passport, matches and plan, and doesn't invent jobs, companies or statistics. When the AI service isn't connected, the app says so and everything above still works.

Why not just ask ChatGPT or Claude?

You can, and a chatbot will give you ideas. What it won't give you is the same structured comparison every time, built from your own evidence and remembered between visits:

  • Structured career paths: every recommendation is checked against the same list of typical skills for each role.
  • Evidence: each match shows which of your skills and achievements back it up.
  • Transparent gaps: exactly what's missing per role, and which gaps matter across roles.
  • Saved progress: a plan you check off, saved with your Passport.

Honest limits: 8 career paths today, matched by keywords, and some real-world wording won't be recognised yet. Live job listings appear only when a job-search service is connected; until then, each match links to job searches on Indeed, LinkedIn, Glassdoor, TeamWork Online and WorkInSports.
