LJSportMind: product overview

LJSportMind is a web app by LJSOCCER (TikTok: @ljsoccer01). The live app is at https://ljentp.vercel.app. This page describes what the app does in plain text, so it can be read without running the app. Last updated: 20 October 2026.

In one sentence: LJSportMind matches a sports person to sports careers from their Sports Passport, shows what they are missing, keeps a step-by-step plan, and lets them prove skills with the LJ Coach Certification. An optional Body ID scan checks physical fit for coaching and game-day roles.

Who it is for: college students, athletes and former athletes, and early-career people who want to work in the sports industry.

1. One product, one flow

LJSportMind is a single flow on one page:

  1. LJ Body ID: a 20-second standing-still camera scan that becomes the user's identity in the app.
  2. Sports Passport: the user's experience and the skills detected from it, with the Body ID shown as the Passport's identity.
  3. Career matches: the sports roles that fit, each explained with evidence.
  4. Development plan: a saved checklist toward a target role that updates itself as the Passport grows.
  5. Job-posting check: a real posting compared with the user's evidence.

The home page opens with the headline "Advice is everywhere. Proof isn't.", two buttons ("See my career matches" and "Get certified"), an example LJ Coach credential, three cards (Career match, Proof, Your plan) and a table titled "Why not just ask an AI chatbot?" comparing an AI chatbot with LJSportMind: both give career ideas; a chatbot only partly saves your progress; only LJSportMind has graded certification levels, a certificate with a credential ID for LinkedIn, levels that update your matches and plan, and a physical-fit check for coaching and game-day roles. The optional Body ID section comes after the Passport and matches.

2. LJ Body ID

The Body ID is optional. It matters for coaching and game-day roles, where the body is part of the job. Once scanned, it is shown on the Sports Passport card.

How the scan works

  1. The user props their phone up at waist height and steps back 2–3 metres so they are visible from head to feet.
  2. Step 1 (front): "Face the camera. Feet hip-width apart, arms relaxed. Stand still." The scan waits until the body is in view, counts down 3-2-1, then records 7 seconds.
  3. Step 2 (side): "Turn sideways to the camera. Stand still." Another 7 seconds.
  4. Only time spent in view counts. If the person steps out of frame, the scan pauses instead of wasting time. If the feet are out of frame, it still measures the upper body and says which measurements were missed.

There are no exercises; the person only stands still. The pose AI (Google MediaPipe) runs on the phone. The video never leaves the device; only the numbers are saved, on the device.

What the user gets

  • A Body ID code such as LJ-GZNU-UPV9, and a gold "signature" shape drawn from 8 measurements: shoulder level, hip level, head position, knees, weight balance, stillness, frame (shoulder-to-hip width) and leg-to-torso length.
  • Traits such as "Broad frame", "Level stance" or "Rock-steady".
  • A health watch-list covering spine and posture, neck and head, knees, hips and weight, and balance. Each area is marked "Looks typical", "Worth watching" or "Worth a professional check", with what the scan saw, why it can matter, and who can help (a doctor, physiotherapist or athletic trainer). Reported pain in an area always raises it to "Worth a professional check".
  • Memory: every later scan is compared with the first one, e.g. "Head forward: 19° → 9°".

The Body ID is a screening guide, not a diagnosis. A phone camera can be a few degrees off, so the app talks about patterns and refers people to professionals. It never diagnoses a condition or clears anyone to play.

3. Career Discovery

Step 1: Sports Passport

The user writes what they have actually done, one item per line (for example "Ran the club soccer team's Instagram account"). The app detects skills and interests from that text automatically and shows which line each came from. The user can remove wrong ones or add missed ones. They also choose a role, career goal and education level. A resume (PDF or text) can be imported; it is read on the device and not uploaded.

Step 2: Career matches

The Passport is compared with 8 sports career paths, each with the skills it typically uses: Sports Content Creator, Team Video / Media Analyst, Sports Journalist / Broadcaster, Youth or Club Coach, Scouting / Analytics, Marketing & Sponsorship, Event Operations, and Front Office / Team Operations. The top 3 are shown as "Strong match", "Good match" or "Possible match", with "Covers X of Y skills this role typically uses", the evidence behind each skill, what is missing, next steps, and job-search links (Indeed, LinkedIn, Glassdoor, TeamWork Online, WorkInSports). There are no fake percentages.

Body fit: only the physically demanding roles (Youth or Club Coach, and Event Operations) are checked against the user's latest Body ID; desk roles are not, because a posture scan says nothing about them. If the watch-list flags an area that role leans on, the match says "Worth watching" or "Get these checked by a professional before you commit", adds a body step to the next steps and the plan, and suggests a lighter path with similar skills (Youth Coach to Scouting / Analytics, Event Operations to Front Office). Within the same match strength, a role that needs a professional check first is ranked lower.

Step 3: Development plan

"Make this my target role" turns a match's gaps into a saved checklist. Steps tick themselves when the matching skill appears in the Passport, and returning users see "Welcome back, [name]. Your plan: X of Y steps done."

LJ Coach Certification (proof for coaching and analysis roles)

A three-level soccer test: Level 1 Foundations (principles of play, formations, sessions, laws), Level 2 Tactical analysis (pressing, build-up, transitions, rest defence) and Level 3 Match analysis (xG, PPDA, field tilt, choosing a training fix, plus an 80-word written analysis). Each level has 10 questions and unlocks in order, with a 10-minute wait after a failed attempt. Level 1 needs 8 of 10. Levels 2 and 3 are very hard and need 9 of 10: Level 2 asks about famous professional moments (2022 World Cup final, Liverpool 4–0 Barcelona 2019, Istanbul 2005, Real Madrid 2–6 Barcelona 2009, Brazil 1–7 Germany 2014, Barcelona v Inter 2010), using only facts that published match reports agree on, with a link to search for the official highlights; Level 3 tests analyst reasoning that also applies to non-sports analyst jobs (regression to the mean, Simpson's paradox, survivorship bias, confounding, Goodhart's law, base rates, small samples and advising decision-makers). Wrong answers are written to be as detailed as the right one, so answer length gives nothing away. Passing a level adds the skills it proves (the Coaching and Data & Analytics skills, and the Player Development and Scouting & Performance Insight interests) to the Passport, so they count in career matches and tick plan steps; the matching evidence reads "LJ Coach Certification Level N passed (score/10)". The Youth Coach, Scouting / Analytics and Team Video / Media Analyst matches show the proof or invite the user to take the check, and those plans include the next level as a step. Each pass issues a credential ID (e.g. LJC2-XXXX-XXXX), a certificate image listing the certified skills, and an "Add to LinkedIn" button that fills in LinkedIn's certification form. After a pass, the app shows what the certification unlocks: the certified skills and the career options they support (Youth or Club Coach, Scouting / Analytics, Team Video / Media Analyst), each with its current match strength, a button to make it the target role, and job searches. It is an LJSportMind skill check, not an official coaching licence, and the app says so. Questions are in English and Spanish.

4. What it adds beyond asking ChatGPT or Claude

  • A measured, private body identity: the Body ID is measured by the phone camera, on the device, and every later scan is compared with the first one. A chatbot cannot take or remember these measurements.
  • The same structured comparison every time: every match is checked against the same list of skills for each career path, with the evidence shown, instead of a new free-form answer each time.
  • Memory and progress: the Passport, Body ID and plan are saved, so the user sees what changed since their first visit and which plan steps are done.

Whether this is enough for users to choose LJSportMind over a chatbot is still being tested with customer interviews.

5. Languages

The whole app, including the Body ID scan and career matches, works in 10 languages: English, Spanish, French, Portuguese, German, Italian, Chinese, Japanese, Hindi and Arabic (right-to-left). Extra languages load only when chosen.

6. Pricing

Free for athletes. Schools and clubs would pay for a team license; a staff view of anonymous progress and certified skills is planned, not built. School pricing is a hypothesis being tested with athletic departments, and payments are not live.

7. Privacy

  • The Passport, plan and Body ID are saved on the user's device.
  • Camera video and resumes are processed on the device and never uploaded.
  • AI requests (when enabled) send only the fields needed to answer and are not stored.

8. Honest limits

  • Career matching uses keywords and 8 career paths; it is guidance, not a hiring prediction. Automatic skill detection reads English text best.
  • The Body ID is a screening guide from a phone camera, not a medical assessment.
  • AI features are switched off until a valid AI key is connected.
  • Translations beyond English were written with AI and should be reviewed by native speakers.
